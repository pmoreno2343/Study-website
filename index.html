Day 1 — Emergency Medicine I

High yield

Goal: recognize unstable patients quickly and know the first life-saving action.

1) ABCDE approach

A — Airway: Can the patient speak? Look for obstruction, stridor, facial burns, trauma. Protect cervical spine if trauma suspected.

B — Breathing: RR, SpO₂, chest movement, breath sounds. Treat tension pneumothorax immediately if suspected.

C — Circulation: Pulse, BP, capillary refill, bleeding, IV access, ECG. Control major hemorrhage first.

D — Disability: GCS/mental status, pupils, bedside glucose.

E — Exposure: Fully examine while preventing hypothermia.

Memory line: Treat life threats as you find them — do not finish the whole exam first.
2) Shock — recognize the pattern
  • Hypovolemic: bleeding/fluid loss → tachycardia, cool skin, low JVP.
  • Cardiogenic: MI/HF → pulmonary edema, raised JVP, cool skin.
  • Distributive (septic/anaphylactic): vasodilation; early septic shock may be warm.
  • Obstructive: PE, tension pneumothorax, tamponade.
Exam rule: shock = impaired tissue perfusion; do not rely on hypotension alone.
3) Sepsis & septic shock

Suspect infection + organ dysfunction. Obtain cultures when feasible, but do not delay antibiotics in a sick patient.

  • Give IV crystalloids for hypoperfusion/hypotension.
  • Start appropriate broad-spectrum antibiotics promptly.
  • If hypotension persists after fluids, norepinephrine is first-line vasopressor.
  • Target MAP is commonly ≥65 mmHg in adults.
Red flag: rising lactate, altered mental status, oliguria, persistent hypotension.
4) Anaphylaxis

First-line: IM epinephrine in the mid-anterolateral thigh. Repeat if needed while supporting airway/breathing/circulation.

Adjuncts: oxygen, IV fluids, bronchodilator for wheeze, antihistamines/steroids for symptoms — but these never replace epinephrine.

Memory line: Anaphylaxis → IM epinephrine first.
5) Altered mental status + hypoglycemia

Immediate bedside checks: ABCs + glucose.

Severe symptomatic hypoglycemia: give rapid glucose (IV dextrose if access; glucagon if IV access is not available).

DOH trap: do not order CT first in every confused patient before checking glucose.
6) Heat illness & dehydration

Heat exhaustion: weakness, sweating, dehydration, usually no severe CNS dysfunction.

Heat stroke: hyperthermia + CNS dysfunction = emergency → rapid active cooling and supportive care.

Memory line: Heat stroke = hot + neurologic dysfunction.
7) Burns — first priorities
  • Stop the burning process; ABCDE.
  • Airway concern if facial burns, soot, hoarseness, stridor, enclosed-space fire.
  • Estimate burn size (TBSA) for significant burns.
  • Large burns need fluid resuscitation; monitor urine output.
Red flag: suspected inhalation injury → early airway planning.
8) Trauma basics

Use ABCDE with cervical-spine protection. Control catastrophic hemorrhage, identify chest threats, assess neurologic status, and expose fully.

Do not forget secondary survey only after immediate life threats are addressed.

Day 1 ExamCure target: 30–40 emergency questions. For every wrong answer, record: clue → diagnosis → first action.
