Day 1 — Emergency Medicine

Topic 1: ABCDE approach to the unstable patient

📅 Day 1

🎯 Definition

ABCDE is the systematic approach to any acutely ill or unstable patient.

Key principle: Treat life-threatening problems immediately when you find them.

A

Airway

  • Can the patient speak normally?
  • Look for stridor, gurgling, snoring, foreign body, facial/neck trauma, burns, hoarseness.
  • In trauma: protect cervical spine.
Immediate actions
• Open airway
• Suction / remove obstruction
• Airway adjunct if needed
• Prepare for intubation if threatened
B

Breathing

  • Assess RR, SpO₂, chest expansion, breath sounds, work of breathing, cyanosis.
  • Identify life threats: tension pneumothorax, severe asthma, pulmonary edema, massive PE.
Key rule
If tension pneumothorax is suspected → treat immediately. Do not wait for chest X-ray.
C

Circulation

  • Assess HR, BP, pulses, capillary refill, skin temperature, JVP, major bleeding, ECG.
  • Gain IV/IO access; send labs; give fluids/blood products as appropriate.
Important
Control hemorrhage.
Hypotension is a late sign of shock; normal BP does not exclude poor perfusion.
D

Disability

  • Assess consciousness/GCS, pupils, limb movement, seizures.
  • Always check bedside glucose in any confused or unconscious patient.
Key rule
Altered mental status → check glucose early. Do not jump to CT before glucose in an unstable patient.
E

Exposure

  • Expose fully enough to look for rash, bleeding, trauma, burns, infection source, swelling.
  • Prevent hypothermia, especially in trauma.
Key points
Examine the whole patient.
Look for hidden injuries.
Keep the patient warm.

🔥 High-Yield Points

  • Treat life threats immediately.
  • Airway obstruction kills first.
  • Tension pneumothorax → immediate treatment.
  • Hypotension can be a late sign of shock.
  • Always check glucose in altered mental status.
  • Prevent hypothermia during exposure.

🧠 Memory Line

Airway → Breathing → Circulation → Brain → Body

“Find a life threat → treat it → then continue ABCDE.”

⚠️ Common Traps

  • Chest X-ray first in suspected tension pneumothorax.
  • CT brain before checking glucose.
  • Finishing the entire exam before intervention.
  • Ignoring cervical spine protection in trauma.
