1. ABCDE Approach

Foundation

Definition: Systematic assessment of any acutely ill or unstable patient.

A — Airway

Can the patient speak? Look for stridor, obstruction, facial burns, hoarseness. Protect C-spine in trauma.

B — Breathing

RR, SpO₂, chest movement, breath sounds. Tension pneumothorax → treat immediately.

C — Circulation

Pulse, BP, cap refill, bleeding, JVP, ECG, IV/IO access. Hypotension may be late.

D/E

D: GCS, pupils, bedside glucose. E: expose fully while preventing hypothermia.

Memory: Airway → Breathing → Circulation → Brain → Body.

2. Shock

Very high yield

Definition: Inadequate tissue perfusion causing cellular hypoxia and organ dysfunction.

Hypovolemic

Hemorrhage/fluid loss. Cool skin, tachycardia, low JVP. Treat source + volume/blood as appropriate.

Cardiogenic

MI/severe pump failure. Raised JVP, pulmonary edema, cool skin. Avoid indiscriminate large fluid boluses.

Distributive

Sepsis/anaphylaxis. Vasodilation; early sepsis may be warm. Treat cause + fluids; vasopressor if needed.

Obstructive

PE, tension pneumothorax, tamponade. Remove the obstruction / treat cause urgently.

Exam clue: Shock is poor perfusion, not just low BP.

3. Sepsis & Septic Shock

Emergency
  • Suspect infection + organ dysfunction.
  • Take cultures when feasible, but do not delay antibiotics in a sick patient.
  • Give IV crystalloids for hypoperfusion/hypotension.
  • If hypotension persists after fluids → norepinephrine is first-line vasopressor.
  • Typical adult MAP target: ≥65 mmHg.
Red flags: rising lactate, altered mental status, oliguria, persistent hypotension.

4. Anaphylaxis

Must memorize

First-line treatment: IM epinephrine in the mid-anterolateral thigh.

  • Support airway, breathing, circulation.
  • IV fluids for hypotension.
  • Bronchodilator for wheeze as adjunct.
  • Antihistamines/steroids are adjuncts — they do not replace epinephrine.
Memory: Anaphylaxis → IM epinephrine first.

5. Altered Mental Status & Hypoglycemia

Common trap

Start with ABCs + bedside glucose.

  • Severe symptomatic hypoglycemia: rapid glucose replacement.
  • Use IV dextrose if IV access is available.
  • Glucagon is useful if IV access is not available.
DOH trap: Do not automatically order CT before checking glucose.

6. Heat Illness

Pattern recognition
Heat exhaustion

Weakness, sweating, dehydration, dizziness; no severe CNS dysfunction.

Heat stroke

Hyperthermia + CNS dysfunction → emergency requiring rapid active cooling and supportive care.

Memory: Heat stroke = hot + neurologic dysfunction.

7. Burns

Airway first
  • Stop the burning process; then ABCDE.
  • Look for inhalation injury: facial burns, soot, hoarseness, stridor, enclosed-space fire.
  • Estimate TBSA for significant burns.
  • Large burns require fluid resuscitation and urine-output monitoring.
Red flag: suspected inhalation injury → early airway planning.
✍️ My personal notes

8. Trauma Basics

ABCDE + C-spine
  • Use ABCDE with cervical-spine protection.
  • Control catastrophic hemorrhage immediately.
  • Identify chest life threats early.
  • Assess neurologic status.
  • Secondary survey comes after immediate life threats are treated.
Memory: Primary survey saves life; secondary survey finds the rest.
